Buttons

Every action is a button, labeled with a verb and what it acts on ("Add competitor", not "Add" or "Submit"). One primary button per area: the thing most people came to do. Navigation is a link, not a button.

Kinds

Primary: the main action. Default: other actions. Quiet: cancel, low-weight actions in rows. Danger: deletes and other harm.

Sizes

Medium everywhere. Small inside rows, tables and toolbars. Large only on sign-in and empty-state calls to action.

States

Working: the label stays, a spinner joins it, the button is disabled until the answer arrives. Disabled: say why in a tooltip or the hint beside it.

Study at least two companies first
Icon buttons

Only for universal actions, always with an accessible name.

Toggle group

Switching between views of the same data (grid, list). Not for filters: filters go in the sidebar.

Where they go

Page actions top right of the page header, primary last. Dialog actions bottom right, primary last. Row actions at the end of the row; more than two go in a menu.

Do
  • "Add competitor", "Run survey", "Delete project"
  • One primary button in view
  • Buttons for actions, even small ones (Edit, Retry)
Don't
  • "Submit", "OK", "Click here"
  • Two primary buttons side by side
  • Text links that do things

Menus

Actions that are needed now and then sit behind a "More" button: a row's actions beyond the first two, rare page actions, and anything destructive that should not sit next to the main action. Menus never hide the main action.

Select and multi-select

Never the browser's own select: it ignores the app's type and cannot show a description. Options can carry a second line saying what they mean. Long lists get a search box. Multi-select shows checkboxes and a count.

Charts

Bars for amounts over time, lines for trends. Series colors in a fixed order and kept with their thing when filters change. A legend whenever there are two series or more, a tooltip on hover, one axis only. Status colors are never series colors. When a single number says it, use a stat tile instead of a chart.

Tabs and side lists

Tabs split a record into views (Profile, Timeline, Facts) and sit under the page header. The selected tab is in the URL so back and reload return to it. Counts show how much is in each. A long single page gets a side list of its sections instead.

Filters

Filters live in a sidebar on the left of the results: a search box, then groups of checkboxes with counts, then selects for long lists. Every filter is in the URL. A "Clear" button appears once anything is set. Never a row of clickable pills.

Modals and drawers

A modal is for a short task that blocks the page: a small form (five fields or fewer), a confirmation, a choice. A drawer shows a record's detail beside the list it came from, so people can go through items one by one. Anything longer is a page. Escape and the scrim close both; a form with changes asks first.

Confirm only what cannot be undone

Deleting a project asks first and names what goes. Removing a company from a project does not: it shows a toast with Undo.

Delete Seniorverse Day?
The project, its surveys, comparisons and digests are deleted. Its companies and what was collected about them are kept.

Toasts

A toast confirms an action that worked, or offers Undo. It appears at the bottom center, stays six seconds, and never holds anything people must read later. Errors that block work go in the page, next to what failed.

Notices

A notice sits in the page and stays until the situation changes: something is not set up, a run stopped, a record is read-only. Shaded, with an icon and a bold first line. No colored side bar.

Loading and progress

Pages load with skeletons in the shape of what is coming, never a blank page or a centered spinner. Spinners are for small things: a button that is working, a status. Long work shows progress with words ("Page 7 of 20: storiicare.com/pricing") and runs in the background.
